1. What an "advanced capstone project" means in the Romanian state university system

Romanian law and regulations don't use the term capstone. Under the Bologna structure (Law 199/2023 on higher education), the equivalent is the graduation work (lucrarea de finalizare a studiilor), which a student defends in the final exam (examen de finalizare a studiilor):

  • Cycle I (licență), 3–4 years. Students write a lucrare de licență. In engineering programmes it is a proiect de diplomă and leads to the engineer's diploma.
  • Cycle II (master), 1–2 years. Students write a lucrare de disertație (master's dissertation). This is the advanced capstone: it should contain original research or a significant applied innovation.

The national framework is Ministry of Education Order no. 3691/2024, amended by Order no. 7843/2024. Each university applies its own regulation under it, starting with the 2025–2026 academic year. Key rules:

  • The licență/diplomă exam has two parts: (a) an assessment of fundamental and specialty knowledge and (b) a public presentation and defence of the thesis or diploma project.
  • The dissertation exam consists of the public presentation and defence of the dissertation.
  • Defences are held in person. Online defence is allowed only during an alert, necessity or emergency state, and must be fully recorded and archived.
  • Before the defence, the work goes through a similarity (anti-plagiarism) check with a nationally recognised program. The reports go into the exam file, and each university sets its maximum similarity threshold. The supervisor must check that the work meets the standards of original scholarly work.
  • Grades are whole numbers from 1 to 10. Each part needs at least 5.00 and the overall average at least 6.00.
  • Universities publish the proposed topics and bibliography on their website.

How these proposals are pitched. Every proposal is scoped as a Master's dissertation (Cycle II) or as an advanced final-year diploma project (Cycle I) that is research-flavoured and engineering-heavy. Each one needs a working artefact (code, prototype, dataset or model), a rigorous evaluation and a written thesis, and can be defended in public. Many are designed to produce a conference or journal paper.
