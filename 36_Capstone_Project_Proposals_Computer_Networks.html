1. What a capstone project is in Romanian universities

Romanian universities don't use the term "capstone project". The equivalent is the final-year graduation project that ends the bachelor's (licență) cycle. Engineering programmes (such as Computers and Information Technology, 240 ECTS) call it the proiect de diplomă and end with an examen de diplomă. Other programmes call it the lucrare de licență, with an examen de licență.

  • Legal framework. Higher Education Law no. 199/2023 and the graduation framework methodology OME 3.691/2024, amended by OME 7.843/2024. From 2025–2026 onwards, the graduation exam has two compulsory parts: (a) an assessment of fundamental and specialty knowledge and (b) the public presentation and defence of the graduation project. Each university and faculty sets its own detailed rules.
  • Nature of the work. The project is individual and original. It applies knowledge from several courses to a real engineering problem and is completed during the final year under a scientific supervisor (conducător științific). The topic and its basic requirements are recorded in a topic sheet (fișa temei). Topics are published on the faculty website, and the student chooses one at the start of the final year.
  • Typical requirements (example: Faculty of Automation and Computers, Politehnica University of Timișoara). About 40–50 pages, written in Romanian or in the language of the programme. A signed declaration of originality. A similarity check with a maximum of 35% similarity. A practical demonstration of the system is compulsory, and if it works only partly, the grade is reduced in proportion. The supervisor writes an evaluation and validates the project before the defence. The presentation lasts about 7 minutes, followed by questions.
  • Grading (same example). Each committee member gives a whole grade from 1 to 10 for each part. Each part needs at least 5.00, and the average of the two parts needs at least 6.00. There are sessions in June/July, September and February.
  • Implication for this catalogue. Each proposal below is written as a diploma-project topic. It builds on Computer Networks (Weeks 1–7) and draws on programming, operating systems and security knowledge. It is sized for a full final year of individual work.

3. Standard deliverables for every proposal

Deliverable 1: Application (software)

  • Complete source code in a Git repository with a meaningful commit history covering all stages
  • Automated build (Maven/Gradle/Makefile) and automated tests
  • Scripts that rebuild the test setup and reproduce every experiment in the thesis
  • Executables, configuration examples, datasets and capture files (.pcapng) used as evidence
  • README with an architecture overview, installation and quick-start guide
  • A working live demo for the defence (a recorded video is a backup only if the faculty accepts it)

Deliverable 2: Thesis as a Word document (≈40–60 pages, or the faculty's limit)

  1. Cover and title page in the faculty's format
  2. Topic sheet (fișa temei) signed by the student and supervisor
  3. Abstract in Romanian and English (≈1 page) and keywords
  4. Table of contents, list of figures and tables
  5. Introduction: context, motivation, objectives, structure of the thesis
  6. State of the art and theoretical background: standards (RFCs), existing solutions
  7. Requirements and specification: functional and non-functional, assumptions
  8. System design: architecture, protocols, data structures, diagrams
  9. Implementation: technologies, main modules, key algorithms and decisions
  10. Testing and experimental evaluation: testbed, methodology, results, discussion
  11. User and deployment manual
  12. Conclusions, personal contributions (stated explicitly) and future work
  13. Bibliography (consistent citation style, e.g. IEEE) and annexes (selected source code, configurations)
  14. Signed declaration of originality (Declarație de autenticitate / originalitate)
